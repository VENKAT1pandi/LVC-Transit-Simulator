# Legal screen: two instruments pass, with a sponsor problem

Gate G0 · 15 November 2026 · For: Sound Transit finance team · Not legal advice; every point below needs confirmation by counsel.

## Answer

Two value-capture instruments are available in Washington law for the West Seattle stations. Sound Transit cannot levy either one itself. The City of Seattle would have to form them and pass the proceeds through.

| Instrument | Law | Sponsor | Binding limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local improvement district (LID) special assessment | RCW 35.43 | City of Seattle | Assessment capped at special benefit; owners carrying 60% of the cost can stop it (RCW 35.43.180) |
| Tax increment area (TIA) | RCW 39.114 (2021, amended 2026) | City of Seattle | At most $200 million of assessed value per area, two areas at a time, 25-year sunset |

## Why the sponsor matters

RCW 81.112.070 gives a regional transit authority general powers to build and fund a system. It does not mention local improvement districts or special assessments. Both instruments therefore need an interlocal agreement under which the City forms the district or area and commits the revenue to the project. Without that agreement, the gate fails.

## Local improvement district

- **What it charges:** a one-time assessment on each parcel, no larger than that parcel's special benefit from the improvement, payable in installments. Seattle has used LIDs for the waterfront and for the South Lake Union streetcar (to be confirmed).
- **The risk:** a protest by owners subject to 60% or more of the total cost, filed within 30 days of the ordinance, divests the council of jurisdiction (RCW 35.43.180).
- **What the model assumes:** a capture share of 30–60% of the estimated causal land-value gain on single-family parcels within a 10-minute walk, assessed once the gain has been realized, with collection of 95–99%.

## Tax increment area

- **What it captures:** regular property tax levies on assessed value above the base year. Excluded are the state school levy, school excess and EMS levies, and port or PUD debt levies (RCW 39.114.050(1)(c)).
- **The binding limit:** an area may hold no more than $200 million in assessed value, or 20% of the sponsor's total if that is less (RCW 39.114.020(1)(c)(i)). A sponsor may run at most two areas at once ((1)(d)). Filled nearest-first up to the cap, the two areas hold 352 of the 2,591 parcels within a 10-minute walk of Delridge and Alaska Junction ($383 million of 2027 assessed value against $2.62 billion in the full rings). The cap, not the capitalization estimate, sets the TIA's size.
- **Process:** an ordinance after at least two public hearings; no public vote (RCW 39.114.020(8)(a)).
- **Net-new rule:** a TIA collects all growth above its base, including growth that would have happened anyway. The model reports gross and net separately and counts only the causal part as project revenue.

## Instruments screened out

- **One-time betterment levy:** no general statute found. The LID is the closest Washington equivalent.
- **Joint development on agency land:** lawful, but no open data on agency holdings. Phase 2.

## What would change this screen

- A change to RCW 39.114's assessed-value cap, or confirmation that Sound Transit counts as a "local government" under RCW 39.114.010.
- A city council position against forming either instrument.

Sources: RCW 35.43.180, RCW 39.114 (full text) and RCW 81.112.070, from app.leg.wa.gov, retrieved 15 November 2026.
